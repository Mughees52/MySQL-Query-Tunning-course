Writing SQL the optimizer loves

Same result, a tenth of the cost. Filters, joins, and subqueries have shapes — and the optimizer handles some shapes far better than others.

slide 3 · the OR trap
"IN and OR are the same thing."
On one column, yes. Across two columns the OR poisons both indexes — rewritten as a UNION of seeks, it's fast again.

Negation's landmine

The NOT IN null bomb

"Which coupon codes were never used on a cancelled order that day?" Right answer: 15.

NOT IN (subquery)

the subquery's list contains one NULL…
SAVE3SAVE12NULL SAVE7SAVE18
→ 0 rows. No error. No warning.
x <> NULL is unknown — one NULL poisons every row's predicate

NOT EXISTS (correlated)

tests row existence — never compares to NULL
SAVE1SAVE4SAVE9 SAVE11…
→ 15 rows. Immune by design.
or: add IS NOT NULL inside the NOT IN subquery
